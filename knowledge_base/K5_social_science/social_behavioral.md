# Social Psychology & Behavioral Science

## When to retrieve
User discusses workplace dynamics, decision-making struggles, social pressure, comparison, entrepreneurial stress, or systemic/structural factors affecting their well-being.

## Social Comparison & Status Anxiety

### Festinger's Social Comparison Theory
People evaluate themselves by comparing to others. Upward comparison (comparing to those "above") creates motivation but also envy and inadequacy. Downward comparison provides relief but also guilt.

**For high achievers**: Social media and peer networks create constant upward comparison. A founder worth $10M feels poor next to one worth $100M. This is not greed — it's a wired-in evaluation mechanism.

**In conversation**: "You said you feel like you're falling behind. Compared to whom? And who chose that benchmark?"

### Hedonic Treadmill
Happiness returns to baseline after positive events. The promotion, the exit, the new house — the joy fades. This is not ingratitude; it's neurobiology. Understanding this reduces self-blame for "not being happy enough."

**In conversation**: "It makes sense that the excitement faded. That's not a character flaw — it's how the brain works. The question is: what gives you sustained meaning, not just momentary happiness?"

## Entrepreneurial Psychology

### Founder Mental Health Landscape
- 72% of founders report mental health concerns (vs. 48% general population)
- 2x more likely to experience depression
- 2x more likely to have suicidal thoughts
- Loneliness at the top: can't show weakness to investors, employees, or even partners

### Common patterns

**Identity fusion**: When "I am my company" — any business setback becomes a personal identity crisis. Separation of self from venture is critical.

**In conversation**: "Your company had a bad quarter. You had a bad quarter. Those are two different statements. Which one feels more true?"

**Survivorship bias guilt**: "Others have it worse, I should be grateful." Comparing suffering is not helpful. Pain is not a competition.

**In conversation**: "You're allowed to struggle even when things look good from the outside. Having resources doesn't mean having peace."

**Decision fatigue**: By evening, willpower and emotional regulation are depleted. This is when arguments happen, impulse decisions are made, and dark thoughts visit.

**In conversation**: "You mentioned this always hits you at night. That's not coincidence — your decision-making resources are depleted by then. How about we explore this when you're fresh?"

## Behavioral Economics Insights

### Loss Aversion (Kahneman & Tversky)
Losses feel ~2x more painful than equivalent gains feel good. Losing $100 hurts more than finding $100 feels good. This explains why wealthy people still feel anxious — they have more to lose.

**In conversation**: "The anxiety about losing what you've built makes perfect sense. Our brains are literally wired to feel losses more intensely than gains."

### Sunk Cost Fallacy
Continuing something because of past investment, not future value. Common in relationships ("We've been together 5 years"), careers ("I spent 10 years building this"), and projects.

**In conversation**: "You've invested a lot in this. But the question isn't 'how much have I put in?' — it's 'if I were starting fresh today, would I choose this?'"

### Choice Overload (Barry Schwartz)
More options → more anxiety → less satisfaction. High-achievers with unlimited options often feel more paralyzed than constrained people. The paradox of freedom.

**In conversation**: "Having infinite options sounds like freedom, but it can feel like a prison of indecision. What if you only had two choices?"

## Systemic Awareness

### Intersectionality
People's experiences are shaped by overlapping identities — gender, race, culture, class, sexuality. A Chinese man in Singapore navigates different pressures than in London. Acknowledge context without making assumptions.

### Cultural code-switching
Bilingual/multicultural individuals often feel fragmented — one self in Chinese, another in English. This is not inauthenticity; it's adaptation. Help users integrate rather than choose.

**In conversation**: "You mentioned feeling like a different person in Chinese vs. English. That's not being fake — that's being multidimensional. The question is: which version feels most like home?"
