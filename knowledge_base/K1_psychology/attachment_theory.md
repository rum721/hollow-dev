# Attachment Theory

## When to retrieve
User discusses relationship patterns, fear of abandonment, difficulty with intimacy, trust issues, or recurring relationship conflicts.

## Core concept
John Bowlby & Mary Ainsworth: Early bonds with caregivers form "internal working models" that shape all adult relationships. These patterns are not destiny — they can be understood and gradually shifted through awareness and corrective experiences.

## Four adult attachment styles

| Style | Self-view | Other-view | In relationships | Core fear | Triggered by |
|:---|:---|:---|:---|:---|:---|
| Secure | Positive | Positive | Comfortable with intimacy and independence | — | — |
| Anxious-Preoccupied | Negative | Positive | Craves closeness, fears abandonment, hypervigilant to rejection signals | Being left | Delayed texts, emotional distance, ambiguity |
| Dismissive-Avoidant | Positive | Negative | Values independence, suppresses emotions, uncomfortable with closeness | Losing autonomy | Demands for closeness, emotional intensity |
| Fearful-Avoidant | Negative | Negative | Wants intimacy but fears it, push-pull pattern | Intimacy itself | Both closeness and distance feel threatening |

## The anxious-avoidant trap
The most common painful dynamic: an anxious person pursues closeness → the avoidant person withdraws → the anxious person pursues harder → the avoidant withdraws further. Both are acting from fear, not malice.

**In conversation**: "It sounds like the more you reach out, the more they pull back — and the more they pull back, the more urgently you need to reach out. You're both caught in a loop."

## Earned security
Attachment styles are not permanent. Through self-awareness, therapy, and corrective relationship experiences, people can develop "earned security" — understanding their patterns and choosing different responses.

## Application rules
1. NEVER label: Don't say "You have anxious attachment." Instead explore patterns: "Do you notice this feeling of urgency comes up in other relationships too?"
2. Normalize the pattern: "This makes so much sense given what you've described about your childhood."
3. Explore the origin gently: "When was the first time you remember feeling this way?"
4. Both sides have valid needs: The anxious person's need for reassurance is valid. The avoidant person's need for space is valid. The issue is the dynamic, not the individuals.
