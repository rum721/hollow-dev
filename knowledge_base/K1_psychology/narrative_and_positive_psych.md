# Narrative Therapy & Positive Psychology

## When to retrieve
User is stuck in a negative self-story, needs help reframing their narrative, or is in a positive state that can be deepened.

---

## Narrative Therapy (Michael White & David Epston)

### Core principle
The person is not the problem. The problem is the problem. By changing how we narrate our experience, we change our relationship to it.

### Key techniques

**Externalization**: Separate the problem from identity. Not "I am depressed" → "Depression has been visiting me." Not "I'm a procrastinator" → "Procrastination showed up again." This shift restores agency.

**In conversation**: "You said 'I'm broken.' What if we looked at it differently — what if something broke around you, and you've been carrying the pieces?"

**Unique Outcomes**: Find exceptions to the problem story. "You said anxiety always wins. Was there ever a time — even a small one — when it didn't?"

**Re-authoring**: Help construct an alternative story. "You've described yourself as someone who always fails at relationships. But you also just told me about a moment of real courage with your partner. What does that say about you?"

**Witnessing**: Reflect back what you've heard in a way that honors the user's experience. "What I'm hearing is someone who has been fighting very hard, for a very long time, mostly alone."

## Positive Psychology (Martin Seligman)

### PERMA Model of Well-being

| Element | Meaning | AI application |
|:---|:---|:---|
| Positive Emotions | Joy, gratitude, hope, love | Help user savor good moments: "Stay with that feeling for a moment. What does it feel like?" |
| Engagement | Flow states, deep absorption | Explore: "When do you lose track of time? What are you doing?" |
| Relationships | Meaningful connections | "Who in your life makes you feel most like yourself?" |
| Meaning | Purpose beyond self | "What would you want your legacy to be?" |
| Accomplishment | Mastery and competence | "What's something you've done recently that you're quietly proud of?" |

### Savoring technique
When a user shares good news, don't just acknowledge — help them deepen the positive experience. "Tell me more about that moment. Where were you? What did it feel like in your body?" This is the opposite of what most people do (quickly move on from good news).

### Character Strengths (VIA)
24 strengths across 6 virtues (Wisdom, Courage, Humanity, Justice, Temperance, Transcendence). Instead of fixing weaknesses, identify and amplify strengths. "From everything you've told me, one of your core strengths seems to be perseverance — you don't give up, even when it hurts."
