# ACT: Acceptance and Commitment Therapy

## When to retrieve
User is struggling with avoidance, feeling stuck, searching for meaning, or trying to eliminate uncomfortable feelings.

## Core philosophy
Steven Hayes' ACT: The goal is not to feel better, but to get better at feeling. Psychological flexibility — the ability to be present, open up to difficult experiences, and do what matters.

## Six core processes (Hexaflex)

### Cognitive Defusion — Unhooking from thoughts
Not "I am a failure" but "I notice I'm having the thought that I am a failure." This tiny shift creates distance between you and your thoughts.

**Technique**: "Try adding 'I notice I'm having the thought that...' before that statement. How does it feel different?"

### Acceptance — Making room for discomfort
Not wanting, liking, or approving of pain — simply allowing it to be present without fighting it. Fighting pain creates suffering; pain + resistance = suffering.

**In conversation**: "What if this anxiety didn't need to go away for you to do what matters?"

### Present Moment — Being here now
Neither trapped in past regret nor future worry. The only moment you can act in is this one.

**In conversation**: "Right now, in this exact moment — not yesterday, not tomorrow — what's actually happening?"

### Self-as-Context — The observing self
You are not your thoughts, emotions, or stories. You are the awareness that observes them. Like the sky that contains all weather but is not the weather.

**In conversation**: "These feelings are passing through you, but they're not you. You've felt differently before, and you will again."

### Values — Your compass, not your destination
Values are directions, not goals. You can never "achieve" kindness — you can only keep walking toward it. When someone feels lost, values provide direction.

**Key questions**: "What do you want your life to stand for?" "If no one was watching, what would you choose?" "On your deathbed, what would you regret not doing?"

### Committed Action — Moving toward values despite discomfort
Taking concrete steps aligned with values, even when it's uncomfortable. Courage is not the absence of fear — it's action in the presence of fear.

**In conversation**: "What's one small thing you could do this week that moves toward what matters to you — even if it feels scary?"
