# IFS: Internal Family Systems

## When to retrieve
User describes inner conflict, contradictory desires, self-sabotage, or says things like "part of me wants X but another part wants Y."

## Core concept
Richard Schwartz's IFS: The mind is naturally multiple. Everyone has sub-personalities ("parts") and a core Self. No part is bad — every part has a positive intention, even the destructive ones. Healing happens when all parts are seen, heard, and understood.

## Three types of parts

### Managers — The controllers
Proactive protectors that try to prevent pain. Manifest as: perfectionism, people-pleasing, overworking, hyper-vigilance, control, intellectualizing.

**In conversation**: "There's a part of you that works incredibly hard to keep everything together. It's been doing this for a long time. What would happen if it took a break?"

### Firefighters — The emergency responders
Reactive protectors that activate when pain breaks through. Manifest as: binge eating, drinking, doom-scrolling, impulsive spending, rage outbursts, dissociation.

**In conversation**: "When the anxiety gets too intense, something kicks in and you reach for your phone — that part is trying to help you escape the feeling. It's not weakness; it's protection."

### Exiles — The wounded ones
Young, vulnerable parts carrying pain, shame, fear, and traumatic memories. Managers and Firefighters exist to keep Exiles locked away.

**In conversation**: "Underneath all that anger, there might be a younger part of you that felt very alone. Does that resonate?"

## Self energy — The 8 C's
When parts step back, the core Self emerges. Self has: Calm, Curiosity, Clarity, Compassion, Confidence, Courage, Creativity, Connectedness. The goal is to lead from Self, not from parts.

## Application rules
1. Always approach parts with curiosity, not judgment: "What is this part trying to protect?"
2. Never try to eliminate a part — befriend it.
3. Use the user's own language for their parts. If they say "my inner critic," use that term.
4. The question "How do you feel toward that part?" reveals whether the user is in Self (curious, compassionate) or another part (annoyed, scared).
