### Inner Authority — How to make decisions
The most practically useful concept. Each person has a different decision-making center:

**Emotional Authority** (most common): Never decide in the heat of emotion. Wait for the emotional wave to settle, then decide from clarity. "Sleep on it" is literally the strategy.

**Sacral Authority**: The gut response — a visceral yes or no. Ask yes/no questions and notice the body's immediate reaction.

**Splenic Authority**: Instant intuition. The first flash of knowing, before the mind interferes.

**In conversation**: "When you're facing a big decision, what does your body tell you? Is there a gut feeling, or do you need to wait for the emotions to settle?"

## Big Five / OCEAN — The scientific model

### Five dimensions

| Dimension | High | Low | Relevance to emotional support |
|:---|:---|:---|:---|
| Openness | Curious, creative, unconventional | Practical, traditional, routine-oriented | High-O users may benefit from metaphorical/creative approaches |
| Conscientiousness | Organized, disciplined, reliable | Flexible, spontaneous, casual | High-C users may struggle with self-criticism when they "fail" |
| Extraversion | Energized by people, talkative | Energized by solitude, reflective | Introverts may need more space and silence in conversation |
| Agreeableness | Cooperative, trusting, empathetic | Competitive, skeptical, direct | High-A users may struggle with boundaries and people-pleasing |
| Neuroticism | Emotionally reactive, sensitive | Emotionally stable, resilient | High-N users are the core audience — they feel deeply and need support |

### Why Big Five matters for the product
Unlike MBTI, Big Five has strong scientific validity and predictive power. Neuroticism in particular predicts who will benefit most from emotional support tools. High-neuroticism individuals experience emotions more intensely — they're not "broken," they're wired to feel deeply.

**In conversation**: Never use Big Five terminology directly. Instead, normalize: "You feel things deeply — that's not a flaw, it's a capacity. It just means you need better tools for processing."
